The problem

The logs you most need help with are the ones you can't paste into a chatbot.

10k
Thousands of lines, a handful that matterCron chatter and service restarts bury the one 4769 that says someone is roasting your service accounts.
!
Logs are sensitiveUsernames, hosts, internal IPs, command lines. They shouldn't leave the network, let alone go to a cloud LLM.
?
AI sounds sure even when it's wrongA confident summary is worthless to an analyst unless every claim can be checked against the evidence.

So: keep everything local, and make the AI show its evidence.

How it works

From raw logs to a cited answer, all on one machine

1
CollectWindows Event Log, exported .evtx and Prefetch folders (read natively on Linux and macOS), journald / syslog, web and app logs.
2
GroupThousands of lines become a few hundred patterns. Every event keeps its exact position in the original file.
3
Score~65 detection rules, your Sigma rules, attacker-tool signatures, bursts, and "first time on this host".
4
AI triageMy fine-tuned model reviews each pattern: how much it matters, why, and what to do.
5
EvidenceSummary, timeline, ATT&CK, indicators and a report, every claim linked to the line it came from.

Trust

Fluent isn't the same as right. Three guardrails:

E3
Every claim is citedThe summary and every answer cite numbered log lines. One click opens the exact record in the original file.
✓
Claims are verifiedIf the model names a tool that doesn't literally appear in the log, it's dropped. It can't invent evidence.
{{CHECKS}}
Known-answer tests gate every changeFive investigations with known answers, including a healthy machine that must stay quiet, run on every change to rules, prompts or the model.

They already caught a routine LAN login mapped to an ATT&CK technique, a model suggesting "stop sshd" on a machine reached over SSH, and a scan bug that silently overwrote results.
